Host Health panel — sidebar design options

Three directions for the host-metrics rail beside the Deploy Console, all on the existing slate tokens. Each is shown in context at desktop, then tablet (820) and phone (390). Use the switcher to flip every mock between the brief's states: nominal, red storm, PSI unavailable, first sample.

A — Instrument strip

Recommended

A 322 px rail that mirrors the console's section-label rhythm: STATUS, MEMORY, PRESSURE, OFFENDERS stacked top-to-bottom in the brief's priority order. Nothing hidden behind tabs or drawers — every slot from the data contract is visible at once, the rail scrolls independently of the console.

Why — glanceable in one fixation per section; zero interaction to reach the offenders table; degrades to a 2-column band on tablet and a single stack on phone with no pattern change.
Watch out — the rail is tall; on short viewports the offenders table is below the fold of the rail (it scrolls). Main column narrows to ~740 px on desktop — container rows reflow, they were designed as rows.

B — Glance + drawer

A 302 px rail split in two: a pinned glance card (pill, used-% readout, swap, OOM count) and a tabbed lower half for Pressure / Processes. On tablet the panel becomes a slide-over drawer; on phone, a peeking bottom sheet.

Why — narrowest desktop footprint; the glance answers priorities 1–2 (headroom, health) with zero scroll; drawer/sheet scale gracefully on touch.
Watch out — offenders sit behind a tab (priority 4 costs a click), and a closed drawer/sheet means the host is invisible — risky for a panel whose whole point is memory pressure.

Tablet · slide-over drawer (shown open; closed by default)
Phone · bottom sheet peek

C — Ledger readout

Same stack as A, but typeset as a terminal ledger: labels left, values right in mono, hairline meters, no sub-panels. ~20% denser than A — all twelve offenders and every PSI number fit without scrolling on a 900 px-tall display.

Why — maximum density with minimum chrome; matches the ops-console DNA; pill idiom still carried for health so it doesn't read as raw syslog.
Watch out — dense mono rows are slower to scan for non-terminal folks; the memory bar loses the big numeric readout's prominence. Same band/stack responsive strategy as A.
